Ledger

Ledger is flat. There is no elevation anywhere in it, not on the header that stays put while you scroll and not inside a tooltip. What separates one thing from another is a rule, a gap, or the single step from the page floor up onto a card. It is warm rather than clinical, comfortable rather than tight, and its dark theme sits on pure black, so on the phone the coach actually uses the unlit pixels of an evening session are genuinely off.

The trade-off in one sentence. Ledger is the calmest and the least tiring to look at for hours, and the price is that on a dense screen it separates things a little less sharply than the other two.

  • Palettehouse-sepia. Warm paper in light, pure black in dark, burnt sienna accent.
  • DensityComfortable. 48px rows, 24px gutters, generous section gaps.
  • Tonal weightThe lowest of the three. No shadow is drawn anywhere.
  • LaptopA permanently expanded 248px sidebar. Never collapsible.
  • PhoneA 56px bottom bar. The same five destinations, same order, same words.

The phone frames are the same markup as the laptop frames, cloned into a narrower container at load: the layout rules key off the width of the application frame, not the width of the window, so what you are looking at is one design at two widths and never a second, different design.

The session runner

The screen the application exists for, and the one used with a client waiting. It is genuinely dense: the current exercise and what is coming next, a jump to any exercise rather than only forward, pause, skip, repeat, reorder and substitute, the previous session at a glance, somewhere to record a reading or a note mid-session, three clients in one session each keeping their own numbers, and the intensity patterns that reshape the whole session on a press. Ledger keeps it legible by hierarchy and by letting the secondary column carry the history, not by throwing anything away.

If a pattern cannot be filled

A routine of nine exercises holds only so many at each level. If a shape you built yourself asks for more hard work than this routine has, the session says plainly which level ran short and what it put in the gap. It does not quietly substitute a different intensity or hand you a shorter session than you asked for.

The diet week

The other genuinely dense screen, and the one where a week grid usually collapses on a phone. Ledger does not shrink the words and does not drop a day. On a laptop it is the whole grid by hour and by day. On a phone it is one day at a time with the whole week permanently in reach above it, and the full grid is still one tap away in a scroller with the hour column pinned. The synchronisation strip is showing the offline state here, which is the application working exactly as designed rather than a fault.

The navigation surface, laptop

Two layers that never overlap, shown working together rather than separately. The permanent sidebar carries the five destinations, always expanded, never collapsible, with the current one filled and marked by a 3px bar down its leading edge so the selection is a shape as well as a tint. Inside the content, the contextual layer carries the to-and-fro: a breadcrumb saying where you are and a way back that says where it goes. A destination never appears in the contextual layer and a contextual action never appears in the sidebar, so the two never disagree about where you are.

Laptop, 248px sidebar

Every screen here reaches every other one. The labelled way back and the contextual links at the foot of each screen mean the browser back button is never the only way out of anywhere, and no screen is a dead end.

The navigation surface, phone

The same five destinations, the same order and the same words, as a 56px bottom bar with the icon above the label and room reserved beneath it for the home indicator. The selected marker moves to the top edge of the destination but stays the same 3px accent bar, because the shape half of the selected state must not change between devices. The synchronisation strip sits directly above the bar, where it is impossible to miss and still blocks nothing.

The synchronisation indicator, every state

Permanently visible, never a modal, and it never blocks anything. Five of these are rungs on a time ladder and they resolve themselves. The sixth is not a rung at all: a rejected change is a difference of kind rather than of degree, because a delay resolves itself when a connection or a sign-in returns, and a rejection resolves itself never and needs a person. Folding it into the ladder would leave a permanently lost change sitting at the bottom of the ladder reading as perfectly healthy.

Tooltips, open

Below the target, on the raised surface, eight pixel corners, no arrow, and flat like everything else in Ledger. Two hundred milliseconds to open and none to close, and they open on keyboard focus as well as on hover. They are written in the coach's words and they say what the thing does for him. Nothing needed to finish a task ever lives only inside one. All three below are shown open here and each is also shown open in place on the screen it belongs to.

On the synchronisation strip, on every screen.

Everything you do is saved on this device the moment you do it, whether you have a connection or not. Backed up means a copy has also reached your own Google Drive. It happens when you open the app, when you leave it, when a session ends, and whenever you tap the button. If it has not happened for a while the strip will say so and get louder, but it will never stop you working.

The application mark

Ledger's own candidate mark rather than one shared between the three directions, because the mark is the first and smallest piece of the look: it sits on a home screen beside the coach's other applications long before he opens anything. A ruled line with three rising marks standing on it, drawn flat in the direction's own burnt sienna and warm paper. Two colours, one silhouette and no fine detail, so it survives the sixteen pixel size a browser tab actually uses.

16 32 48 64 120, home screen 180, home screen 256

The two home screen samples sit on the raised surface rather than on the page floor, so the mark can be judged against something other than its own background. It is a single file at every size: an operating system renders an installable icon with no stylesheet loaded, which is why this one file carries its two colours literally instead of reading them from the token layer the way every screen above does.
