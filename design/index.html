Choose how the application looks

Three complete visual directions were built for the coaching application. They are not three colour schemes of the same thing: each one is laid out differently, moves differently and has its own colours, and sitting in front of any two of them should feel like sitting in front of two different applications. This page is here so you can look at all three doing the same job and pick one.

Everything below is live. Each panel is the real direction, loaded and drawn by the browser as you look at it, not a picture of it. The controls drive all three at once, so whatever you change, you are always comparing like with like.

They are in alphabetical order. That is on purpose and it is not a ranking. There is a recommendation further down and it says so in large letters.

The same screen, three ways

Start with Running a session. That is the screen the whole application exists for and it is where the three differ most. The panels are shrunk to fit beside each other, which is fair about shape and misleading about how full a screen feels, so open at least one of them full size before you decide. One at a time above shows a single direction across the whole width, which is much closer to the real thing.

Console

Fits the most on a screen

Console shows you the most at once and needs the least scrolling, and the price is that it looks busier the moment you open it and is more to take in at the start of a long evening.

One thing to know before choosing Console

Console fits more in by making its rows shorter. Some of those rows are forty pixels tall, and this application set itself a floor of forty-four pixels for anything you tap while a client is talking to you. A row that is itself a link cannot be both. This was left as it is deliberately: making the rows taller would remove the one thing that makes Console different and turn it into Ledger with other colours, which would leave you comparing two of the same thing. Choosing Console means accepting that a few of the rows you tap during a session are slightly smaller than the rest of the application promises.

Ledger

Calmest to look at

Ledger is the calmest to look at for hours and the easiest to talk someone through, and the price is that on a very full screen it separates one thing from the next a little less sharply than the other two.

One thing to know before choosing Ledger

Ledger has no shadows and no raised edges anywhere. Everything is held apart by lines, by space, and by one small step in background colour. That is exactly what makes it restful to sit in front of, and it is the same property that will make your fullest screen harder to take in at a glance than either of the other two would. The session screen is the densest thing in this application and it is the one you will be looking at with a client waiting, so this is not a small point. It is a permanent property of the direction rather than something that can be repaired later, and it is the price of choosing Ledger in the same way the other two have theirs.

Separately, and unlike the two above, this one has been fixed. The worst single problem found anywhere in this set was in Ledger: on a phone, one exercise name in the session screen was breaking across four lines. You are looking at the repaired version. It is named here rather than quietly left out, because a recommendation you can check is worth more than one you cannot.

Roster

Fastest for moving between clients

Roster keeps the list of clients on screen the whole time so moving between people is fastest and you never lose your place, and the price is a permanently narrower working area on the laptop and one more moving part to learn.

One thing to know before choosing Roster

The narrow strip of five icons down the left is almost colourless by design. Looked at on a real screen it does read as part of the application rather than as grey furniture, but only because one of the five is always lit up. Cover the lit one and it is five grey shapes on a grey floor, so it would go flat on any screen where nothing is selected. Roster's own specification says its icons are drawn slightly heavier to stop exactly that; at real size that extra weight cannot be seen and is doing none of the work it was given. It passes, and it passes on one thing.

A recommendation, not a decision

If it were up to us, we would choose Ledger

The way this application is most likely to fail is not by missing a feature. It is by becoming tiring. A tired coach with a client waiting starts making small mistakes, and then quietly stops using the thing. Ledger is the one built against that: warm rather than clinical, roomy rather than tight, and the least glaring of the three in both themes. Its dark theme sits on true black, which on the iPhone you would actually be using means the screen in an evening session is genuinely dark rather than dark grey.

It also asks the least of you. Nothing in it expands when the mouse passes over it, there is no second permanent panel, and nothing behaves differently depending on where the pointer happens to be. That matters more than it sounds, because you will be learning this over a video call, and every moving part is something that has to be explained rather than noticed.

It is not free, and the note beside it above says so at full weight: the flatness that makes Ledger restful is the same thing that makes its busiest screen harder to parse in a glance. We think that is the right trade for a three-hour evening. You may not, and there are two specific reasons to overrule us. Console is the right answer if, having looked at the session screen and the diet week with real content in them, fitting more on one screen looks like the real problem to solve. Roster is the right answer if moving between clients turns out to be what you spend the day doing; it is clearly the best of the three at that and the most expensive at everything else.

One thing that is not a trade-off: the colours are swappable. If you like one direction's shape and another's colours, that pairing is safe, because all three sets were measured and all three pass. The layouts are not swappable, because each was drawn to suit the colours it ships with.

Things you should know before you choose

None of these counts against any one direction. They are here because finding them out later is worse than reading them now.

What has actually been checked, and what has not

All three have been opened in a real browser, at a laptop width and at a phone width, in both the light and the dark theme, first by the people who built them and then again by an independent pass that went through all three and repaired eight problems. What that means precisely: they were drawn on a screen and looked at. Nobody has run a real session in any of them, and none of them is connected to anything.

A small text size that belongs to the set rather than to any one direction

Ledger and Console both show a trail of small text at the top of the content telling you where you are. That text is smaller than the size this application set for reading and smaller than the size it set for tapping. Nothing is unreachable because of it: every screen has a full-size labelled way back and that was checked on every screen. It was left alone because fixing it in one direction and not the other would make those two differ on something that is not part of the choice you are making. It is one type-size decision to settle once you have chosen, and it counts against neither of them.

The icon the application installs with is still a placeholder

If you install the application to a phone today you get a plain flat placeholder icon. That is expected sequencing rather than something left unfinished: the real icon is generated from whichever of the three marks above you pick, so it could not be made before this choice. Nothing else in the application is waiting on it.

What happens next

Nothing else gets built until you choose. That is deliberate, not a pause for approval. Every remaining screen in the application - the clients, the calendar, the routines, the diet week, the admin panel, the setup guide - is built inside whichever of these three you pick. Changing it afterwards is not a small revision; it is repainting every screen in the rest of the project. So take as long as you want over this one, and it is the last thing anyone needs from you before the building resumes.
