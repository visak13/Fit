Console

Console fits noticeably more on a screen than the other two directions do, without shrinking a single word, and it uses elevation rather than dividers to say which surface you are working on. It reads as an instrument. Density is bought entirely from row height and spacing: reading text never drops below sixteen pixels and nothing tapped during a session is under forty-four.

The trade-off in one sentence. Console shows the most in one glance and suits the dense screens best, and the price is that it feels more like an instrument panel than a notebook, which is more to take in at the start of a long session.

What you are looking at. Real seeded content. The routine is Pull Deadlift Back and Biceps as the application ships it, with the nine exercises' own prescriptions, rest intervals, coaching cues and intensity levels, and the shipped intensity patterns. Only the client names and their readings are invented, and a client record here holds exactly what the real one holds: a name, general notes, and an optional non-clinical adaptation flag with a pointer to where the real detail lives elsewhere.

Navigation, laptop

Two layers that never overlap. The rail carries the five destinations and nothing else; the breadcrumb and the labelled back control carry the to-and-fro and never a destination. The rail rests at seventy-six pixels and expands on hover or keyboard focus, holding open while focus is anywhere inside it. That expansion is Console's most obvious moving part, and it is a real part of its trade-off rather than a flaw: it is what buys the second column on the dense screens.

Synchronisation status, every state

Six states, and the sixth is not a sixth rung. The ladder is about TIME and has five steps: saved, waiting, waiting long enough to notice, waiting long enough to act on, and working from the copy on this device with no connection. Rejected sits outside the ladder because it differs in kind: a delay resolves itself when a connection or a sign-in returns, and a rejection never does — it needs a person. Folding it into the ladder would park a permanently lost change at the bottom step, reading perfectly healthy.

Working without a connection is not a failure and is not drawn as one. This application saves to the device first and backs up second, so training a client in a basement gym is the application working exactly as intended. An indicator that cries wolf on an ordinary Tuesday is one he stops reading.

Saved — rounded square
Waiting — pill
Waiting long enough to notice — upright rectangle, from six hours
Waiting long enough to act on — hard-cornered square and a heavy edge, from a day
Working from this device, no connection — wide dashed bar, deliberately calm
Refused — outside the ladder, hollow hard square

Console's own hazard, named rather than rounded off. This is the only direction whose indicator spends most of its life in a collapsed seventy-six pixel rail, which is the one place in the whole set with no room for words. Even collapsed it carries a filled shape and a number, never a bare dot — the count is what makes it information rather than decoration. The wording arrives with the rail, and on the phone the words are always present because a slim bar has the width for them.

The same six with the colour taken away

A greyscale screenshot, a colour-blind reader, a phone in sunlight and a compressed video call all lose the colour. The silhouette is what survives, so each state is a different outline before it is a different fill.

Tooltips, open

Right of the target on the laptop, because the rail is the most common trigger; below it on the phone. Two hundred milliseconds in, nothing on the way out, and they open on keyboard focus as well as hover. These three are shown open because they will be read over a video call, where a tooltip nobody knows to hover over is a tooltip nobody reads.

One — the synchronisation chip, which is the thing he will ask about first
Nothing is lost. Everything you have recorded is saved on this device already. This is telling you it has not reached your Google Drive backup for seven hours. Open the app on a connection, or tap here, and it will go.
Two — the intensity pattern, which does more than it looks like it does
Press this and the whole session rearranges to that shape: an easy start, the hardest work in the middle, an easier finish. It changes the order and it changes the repetitions, the timers and the rest — never the weight. You can still change every number afterwards.
Three — the adaptation flag, where a wrong assumption would matter
Write only what changes what you ask her to do, such as "right knee, avoid deep squats". Do not type a diagnosis, a medication or anything from a doctor's letter. Keep that in your own records and point this app at it instead.

Nothing above is only in a tooltip. The flag hint is also printed under the field on the client record, the pattern's effect is stated on the session runner in the note about what a harder point means, and the synchronisation chip's own words say the same thing in shorter form. A tooltip here only ever repeats something at more length.

Candidate application mark

Console's mark, in Console's palette. It is a session shaped over an hour: an easy opening, a build, the peak, then an easier close — the same curve the intensity patterns press onto a routine. Deliberately not a rising chart, because this application never suggests that loads should go up on their own; and deliberately not the shape of any glyph in the application's own family, so it reads as an identity rather than as a toolbar button.

Fit

Everything that carries meaning sits inside a centred safe area, so a circular or a rounded-square mask crops nothing. It was authored as vector source rather than as an image, which is what lets it stay sharp at sixteen pixels and at one hundred and eighty.
